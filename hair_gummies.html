Human Presence
7.0/10
Human presence is visible in several images - slide 3 shows a hand holding a red gummy, slide 4 shows four men demonstrating different hair loss stages with clear facial expressions, and slides 22-23 show a confident young Indian male model with thick hair eating the gummy and holding the product. The model appears to be in the target age range (20s-30s) and projects a confident, aspirational image. However, the model has perfect, thick hair which may not fully resonate with someone experiencing early-stage hairfall anxiety.
Include at least one relatable image of a young man with visible early-stage thinning who looks hopeful or relieved - someone the target persona can identify with emotionally. Consider showing the same model in a 'journey' context rather than only with perfect hair.
Proof Prominence
7.5/10
Slide 5 prominently displays clinical trial registry data with '90%' statistics in large blue text on the left side - '90% men experienced 53% reduction in hair thinning' and '80% men reported their hair felt more voluminous.' The CTRI logo is visible in the bottom right. The percentage numbers are large and readable. However, the stats appear on only one slide and compete visually with the product packaging on the right side.
Make the '90% in 90 days' claim more prominent by displaying it as a bold hero banner in the first 2-3 carousel slides. Use contrasting colors (white/yellow text on dark background) for the statistics. Add a dedicated badge or seal format for the clinical proof that can be repeated across multiple slides.
Ingredient Imagery
6.5/10
Slide 2 shows ingredient imagery with strawberries, almonds, oat pieces, and seeds scattered around the product packaging, with text callouts for Biotin, Zinc, and Vitamin A/C/E. The ingredients look natural and premium. However, the visual connection between these ingredients and hair health is not immediately obvious - they appear decorative rather than functional.
Add visual infographics showing how each ingredient (Biotin, Zinc, Vitamins) directly impacts hair follicle health. Consider a 'cross-section' diagram connecting ingredient to hair root benefit. Make the ingredient visuals larger and more hero-level rather than scattered decoratively.
Before / After
5.5/10
Slide 4 shows four men at different stages of hair loss (Stage 1-4) but this functions more as a 'which stage are you' diagnostic rather than a true before/after transformation. The slides 14-16 show microscopic hair follicle illustrations (fewer to more hairs, healthier follicles) but these are scientific diagrams rather than real human transformations. User review images (slides 28-31) show product packaging in real-world settings but no actual hair transformation results.
Add at least one clear before/after slide showing a real user's hairline or hair density improvement over 90 days. Include timestamps ('Day 1' vs 'Day 90') and keep lighting/angle consistent for credibility. This is the most critical missing element for this persona who is skeptical after failed remedies.
Lifestyle Shots
5.0/10
User-generated content images (slides 28-31) show the product in real-world contexts - airport cafe, desk setup, bedroom with alarm clock and cap. However, these are product-centric shots rather than lifestyle imagery showing the persona's aspirational life. There are no images of a young man confidently at work, on a date, with friends, or running his hands through healthy hair.
Add lifestyle imagery showing the target persona in aspirational situations: confidently presenting at work, styling hair before going out, selfie-worthy moments, or morning routine shots. Show the emotional payoff of solving hairfall - confidence, not just product usage.

Restructure visual hierarchy in hero slides to lead with the pain point ('Seeing hairfall everywhere?') or key result ('90% reduction in 90 days') as the largest text element, with product as supporting visual. Consider removing price-focused banners from hero carousel in favor of benefit-led messaging.
